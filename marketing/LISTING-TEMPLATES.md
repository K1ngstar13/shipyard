# Store copy that works

Fill-in templates and the reasoning behind them. All limits are enforced by preflight, so you'll hear about it before a store does.

## Where the words actually go

| Field | Limit | Where it shows | What it's for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Name | 30 | Everywhere | Searched heavily. Real words beat clever ones. |
| Subtitle (Apple) | 30 | Under the name | Searched. Your second-best keyword space. |
| Short description (Google) | 80 | Top of the Play listing | The line that decides whether they scroll. |
| Keywords (Apple) | 100 bytes | Invisible | Pure search. Comma-separated, no spaces. |
| Description | 4000 | Below the fold | Mostly read by people already interested. |
| Promotional text (Apple) | 170 | Above the description | Changeable without a new build. Use it for news. |

Most people decide from the icon, the name, and the first screenshot. Spend your effort there.

## Name

Pattern: `<Concrete noun> <mechanic or vibe>`

- Good: Quackie Pond, QuackDoku, Block Pals
- Weak: Duck Game 2026, Puzzle Master Pro, Ultimate Idle Tycoon

Avoid "Pro," "Ultimate," "Master," "3D," and numbers. They read as shovelware and search poorly.

## Subtitle (Apple, 30 characters)

One clear promise, using words people would actually type.

```
Cozy idle duck pond
Calm logic puzzles daily
Blocks, pets, and combos
```

Not: "The best game ever!!" — no keywords, no promise, and a ranking claim.

## Short description (Google, 80 characters)

Say what the player does, in the first five words.

```
Tap ducks, grow a pond, and unlock a hundred silly hats.
Place ducks by logic. One per row, column, and color. No timers.
Clear lines, feed your pet, beat twelve levels of block puzzles.
```

## Keywords (Apple, 100 bytes)

Rules: comma-separated, no spaces, no words already in your name, no other games' names, singular over plural (Apple matches both), no "game" or "app."

```
idle,clicker,cozy,relax,pond,hat,collect,offline,tap,cute,chill,pet
logic,sudoku,brain,puzzle,quiet,daily,zen,nonogram,solve,think
block,match,combo,line,tile,pet,care,level,arcade,drop
```

Think about what a person types when they don't know your game exists. That's the whole exercise.

## Description

Four blocks. The first two sentences do all the work — Google truncates around 80 characters, Apple around three lines.

```
[What the player does, one sentence, concrete.]

[The loop, 2–3 sentences. What you do, what you get, why you come back.]

[What's in it: counts, modes, progression. Numbers convince.]

[The reassurances: works offline, no ads, no purchases, no account.]
```

Worked example:

```
Quackie Pond is an idle game about one duck and a pond that keeps getting nicer.

Tap your duck to earn quacks. Spend them on lily pads, fountains, and friends
who keep quacking while you're away. Come back later and your pond has been
busy without you.

Over 100 hats and outfits to collect, a dozen pond upgrades, and duck skins
that change how your quacks add up.

Plays entirely offline. No ads, no purchases, no account, and nothing about
you leaves your phone.
```

That last paragraph is worth more than it looks. "No ads" is one of the most persuasive things a small game can say in 2026.

## Promotional text (Apple, 170 characters)

Editable any time without a review. Use it for what's new or what's seasonal.

```
New this week: the winter hat set, plus a pond that freezes over at night.
```

## What's new

Say what changed, in plain words. "Bug fixes and performance improvements" tells a returning player nothing.

```
Ducks now waddle when idle. Fixed a bug where hats vanished after closing
the game. Three new pond backgrounds.
```
