# Mach Golf — positioning and launch

## The wedge

Speed training works and the evidence is strong. The problem is that it's been packaged
as a $200 stick set, a $500 launch monitor, a $150/month coach, or a $30/month app that
locks a PDF behind a paywall. The training itself costs nothing. That's the gap.

**One line:** Swing speed training without the $600 of gear.

**Alternative lines to test:**
- Distance is trainable. The price tag wasn't.
- Six weeks. Three sticks you build for $25. More carry.
- The speed program, minus the speed-program industry.

## Who it's for

The 8–20 handicap who watched the field pass him. He's 38, plays twice a month, has
a gym membership he half-uses, and has already priced a stick set and hesitated. He does
not want a coach. He wants a plan and a number that moves.

Secondary: high-school and college players whose programs can't fund equipment, and
golf fitness trainers who need something to hand clients between sessions.

## App Store listing

**Title:** Mach Golf: Swing Speed Training
**Subtitle:** Overspeed, gym and tempo in one

**Description:**

Golf turned into a distance game and the training to keep up got expensive. Mach is the
whole system in one app: a 6-week speed block, the gym work underneath it, a tempo
trainer, and a log that shows you whether any of it is working.

- Six-week program, 3 speed sessions and 2 gym sessions a week, about 35 minutes a day
- Four overspeed protocols with a guided player — implement, reps, cue, rest timer
- 27 gym exercises built for rotational power, each with why it matters and a no-equipment substitute
- Three-beat tempo metronome at a tour 3:1 ratio, adjustable to your swing
- Speed log with your gain over baseline and estimated carry
- Build your own speed sticks for about $25 — the app shows you how
- Works offline. No account. No subscription.

Not affiliated with any equipment manufacturer.

**Keywords:** swing speed, golf distance, overspeed, clubhead speed, golf fitness,
golf tempo, driver distance, golf training, speed sticks, golf workout

## Launch sequence

**Week 1 — build in public.** Post the DIY stick guide on its own to r/golf and
r/GolfSwing as a free thing, no link. It's genuinely useful and it's the most shareable
asset you have. Answer every comment.

**Week 2 — the app, as a follow-up.** "A bunch of people asked what program I run with
those. Here it is, free, no signup." Link it. The sequencing matters: the value came
first.

**Week 3 — YouTube and TikTok.** One format repeated: a 40-second clip of a single
protocol set, the cue on screen, your radar number at the end. Title pattern:
"Overspeed session, week 4 — 104.1 mph." Speed content performs because the number is
the hook.

**Ongoing — your own 6 weeks, in public.** Run the block yourself and post the weekly
retest. A founder with a moving number is the whole marketing plan. Real gains in this
range are roughly 3–7% over 6 weeks, so set expectations there and let the honesty be
the differentiator.

**Partners worth an email:** pocket-radar retailers (you drive demand for the one piece
of gear you recommend buying), indoor sim bays (they want reasons for repeat bookings),
club fitters, and high-school golf coaches.

## Money, if you want it

Do not subscribe people. The pitch is "everything else is expensive" and a subscription
walks straight into it.

- **Free:** the full 6-week block, gym library, tempo trainer, log. Everything in this build.
- **One-time unlock, $14.99:** blocks 2 and 3, video tempo capture, CSV import from
  launch monitors, unlimited saved profiles.
- **Affiliate:** the pocket radar recommendation, the ankle weight, the alignment sticks.
  Disclose it in the app, not in a footer.
- **Coach license, $99/yr:** a trainer assigns the block to clients and sees their logs.
  This is where the real revenue is if the consumer side gets traction.

A 2% conversion on 50,000 free installs at $14.99 is about $15,000. That's the shape of
the business — it's a good side income, not a venture case, and pricing it honestly is
what gets you the installs in the first place.

## Things not to claim

Don't promise mph. Don't imply affiliation with SuperSpeed, Stack, Rapsodo or anyone
else. Don't use "clinically proven." The research supports overspeed training in
general; your app is not the study. Say what the training does and let the user's own
log make the argument.
