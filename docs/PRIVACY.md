# Privacy, per app

A company-wide policy can't do this job on its own. Apple and Google ask about **each app
separately**, and the answers have to match the binary you submit. A blanket policy that
says "we collect nothing" while one of your apps runs analytics is a rejection on Apple
and a policy strike on Google.

So Shipyard treats privacy as a property of each game.

## What happens

**Step 5 of the upload wizard** asks what the app does. Before you answer, Shipyard reads
the game's own code and ticks what it finds: analytics, ads, crash reporting, sign-in,
location, camera or microphone, contacts, messaging, purchases, and whether it talks to a
server at all. You correct anything it got wrong and name any third-party services.

**On every build** that produces three things:

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `games/<slug>/store/privacy.html` | The app's own privacy page, committed to your repo |
| `games/<slug>/store/privacy-answers.md` | The exact answers for both store questionnaires |
| `/apps/<slug>/privacy.html` on your site | The live page the stores check |

**Preflight refuses to submit** when the answers and the code disagree. If the game
contains Google Analytics and the answers say no analytics, the build stops and says so.
That check is the point of the whole feature — it catches the mistake before a reviewer
does.

## The three layers

1. **Company-wide policy** at `elevatr.llc/privacy.html` — who operates the apps, how to
   contact you, retention, deletion, children. Written once, by hand.
2. **Shared practices** — the behaviour common to all your games.
3. **Per-app pages** — generated by Shipyard, one per game, each stating exactly what that
   app does and doesn't do. Each page links back to the company policy and says it takes
   precedence for that app.

Every build also writes **`app-privacy-links.html`**, a paste-in block linking each app's
page. Drop it into the company policy page so `elevatr.llc` stays the front door and the
per-app detail is one click away.

## Putting the pages on your own domain

By default the pages live on the site Shipyard builds. If your website is a GitHub repo,
Shipyard can write them straight into it instead, so the live URL is on your own domain:

1. Publisher sheet → **Website repo** → `owner/repo` for your website.
2. Add that repo to your fine-grained token's repository list (Contents: read and write).
3. Ship. Each build writes `apps/<slug>/privacy.html` into that repo and rebuilds
   `apps/index.html` listing every app. Your host redeploys as usual.

The live URL becomes `https://yourdomain/apps/<slug>/privacy.html`, and that's what goes
in both stores. Builds skip the write when nothing changed, so you don't get a commit per
build. Change `siteRepoPath` in `shipyard.config.json` if you'd rather they sit somewhere
other than `/apps`.

**If your website isn't a GitHub repo** — a site builder like Squarespace or Wix, say —
leave Website repo empty. The pages stay on the Shipyard site, which both stores accept,
and you paste `app-privacy-links.html` into your company policy page so your domain still
links to each one.

## What to give the stores

Use the per-app URL as the privacy policy link in **both** stores:

```
https://your-site/apps/<slug>/privacy.html
```

Not the company-wide one. Reviewers follow that link and check it against your
questionnaire answers; the per-app page is the one that matches.

Then open `privacy-answers.md` for that game and work through it. For an app that collects
nothing, Apple's App Privacy is a single "Data Not Collected" and Google's Data safety is
"No" to the first question — the whole thing takes two minutes.

## When a game changes

Add analytics or ads to a game later and the answers are wrong until you update them. Open
the game in the panel, change step 5, and ship. The page and the answers regenerate, and
preflight catches you if you forget. Update the store questionnaires at the same time —
those live in the consoles and no API can change them.
